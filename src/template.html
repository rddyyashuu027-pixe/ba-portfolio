How I work

How a project usually goes for me

It almost always starts with someone saying "this number looks bad." I try not to jump straight to a dashboard. I'd rather find out what's actually happening first, then build only what helps people act on it.

01Frame

"Returns are high" isn't a question yet. I pin down who owns it and what good looks like.

02Measure

Pull the baseline in SQL and get everyone to count the same way.

03Diagnose

Slice it until the pattern shows up. It's usually one segment, not the whole book.

04Size

Before anyone builds anything, I estimate what the fix is worth and where it stops paying off.

05Specify

Write it up so tech can build it: BRD, stories, acceptance criteria, UAT.

06Monitor

Keep watching after launch. Plenty of fixes quietly stop working.

Case study 01 · Payments

About 2.7% of ACH debits were bouncing back. Which of those could we actually stop?

SQLPythonNACHA return codesRule simulationBreak-even623K synthetic entries
Problem

Every return means handling, a retry, and usually an annoyed customer on the phone. Nobody could say which ones were avoidable.

Diagnosis

Two-thirds are insufficient funds (R01), and we can't fix people's balances. But closed and invalid account returns pile up on first debits to accounts nobody checked.

Solution

Check the account once, before the first debit. People who already pay us never notice. It's worth it as long as a return costs us more than $3.85.

Case study 02 · Card operations

Card disputes were slow, but the investigators weren't the problem.

SQLPythonReg E / Reg ZOLS delay modelForecasting57K synthetic cases
Problem

36% of disputes missed the 10-day SLA, about 6% got reopened, and the plan on the table was to hire more investigators.

Diagnosis

Every time we go back to the customer for documents it adds 2.3 days. Every case sent to the wrong queue adds 2.8. Surprisingly, phone claims were the worst for missing docs (43%), worse than the app.

Solution

Ask for the right documents up front, route by reason code, fast-track the 16% of small low-risk claims, and staff to a weekly forecast instead of gut feel.

Case study 03 · Wealth management

The market went up. So why were "conservative" clients suddenly taking growth-level risk?

SQLPythonEquitiesRebalancingSuitability2,500 accounts · $1.75B
Problem

Each client has a model mix (Conservative 35% stocks, Moderate 60%, Growth 80%). Two strong years for stocks, and nobody really knew how far accounts had moved from those targets.

Diagnosis

58% of accounts are more than 5 points off target, and it lines up almost perfectly with how often the adviser rebalances. One stock that went up ~7x quietly became 19% of 524 accounts.

Solution

An automatic drift alert with a ready-made rebalance trade, a separate single-stock alert (rebalancing doesn't fix that one), and a monthly adviser scorecard.
